1. The Institutional Analysis Playbook (How to Best Use the Terminal)

Most retail market interfaces are designed for distraction: flashing quotes, sensory overload, and opinion-driven headlines. SignalLedger was engineered for the opposite: calm, sequence-aware quantitative rigor.

To maximize the utility of the terminal for informed buying, selling, or position rebalancing, adopt the following operational workflow:

1

Screen & Curate with Zero Latency

Type any ticker in the search bar. The client memory cache and persistent proxy deliver instant suggestions. Click the star icon (★) directly inside any search suggestion to immediately bookmark assets to your local watchlist without triggering a page load or losing your cursor position.

2

Sort Portfolio Pools by Dispersion Extremes

Open the Watchlist drawer and use the segmented sort controls. Sort by "Near 52W Low" to identify high-quality stocks trading at deep cyclical discounts, or by "Near 52W High" to isolate relative-strength momentum leaders breaking out into multi-year highs.

3

Verify the Buy / Sell / Hold Signal & Macro Regime

When loading a stock, immediately glance at the Buy / Sell / Hold Signal Tag (▲ BUY, ● HOLD, ▼ SELL) beside the ticker and the Market Regime Badge beside the chart. Hover over the signal tag to see its multi-factor mathematical breakdown. A BUY signal coupled with a Bullish Regime (> 200-SMA) confirms multi-timeframe institutional backing where pullbacks offer asymmetric upside.

4

Audit the Sequence-Validated Range Ledger

Scroll down to the Yearly High / Low Ledger. Look for flagged rows (* Flagged). If a stock's historical annual ranges are dominated by low-after-high sequences, the reported naive spreads were deceptive selloffs, not tradeable rallies. Check the Revised Next-Year High to see what the low actually recovered to in the subsequent calendar cycle.

5

Layer Overlays for Entry & Exit Timing

Toggle 20 EMA, 50 SMA, and 200 SMA to spot dynamic support confluences. Activate Volume to ensure institutional accumulation behind the current rally. Turn on RSI (14) to confirm the asset is not overextended (> 70) before deploying fresh capital.

The Golden Rule of the Terminal

Never base an investment decision on a single number. A stock trading 10% above its 52-week low is only an attractive entry if (1) it holds above key moving averages or displays bullish RSI divergence, (2) volume confirms institutional accumulation, and (3) historical sequence data shows consistent recovery rallies.

2. First-Principles Guide to Indicators & Overlays

Every tool on this terminal is grounded in fundamental market mechanics, capital flows, and human behavioral psychology. Below is the first-principles breakdown of each indicator, why it works, and how to apply it.

Sequence Check & Revised Next-Year High

Core Architecture
First Principle: Time is Directional, but Naive Spreads are Agnostic. Standard finance portals report annual range as (High - Low) / Low. If a stock opened in January at $100, peaked in February at $130, and collapsed by December to $30, a standard screener reports a "333% range" ((130 - 30) / 30). This creates a lethal cognitive illusion: it implies an investor could have captured a 333% rally, when in reality the stock lost 70% of its value!
Mathematical Condition Valid Rally: Date(Low) ≤ Date(High).
Flagged Sequence: Date(Low) > Date(High).
Correction Mechanism Revised High evaluates the rally from that year's low into the subsequent calendar year's high.
Practical Application: Prioritize businesses that consistently exhibit Low → High ordering across multi-year horizons. When an asset suffers a sequence breakdown during an industry downturn, evaluate the Revised %: if subsequent-year recoveries regularly exceed 50%, you have identified a cyclical compounder with durable mean-reverting elasticity.

Market Regime Indicator (Bullish / Bearish vs. 200-SMA)

Live in Terminal
First Principle: Institutional Mandates Dictate Structural Liquidity. Modern equity markets are not driven by retail traders; they are dominated by quantitative index funds, CTAs, and institutional pensions. Most algorithmic mandates mandate systematic de-risking or prohibited long leverage whenever an asset trades below its 200-day moving average.
Regime Formula Diff % = (Latest Close - 200 SMA) / 200 SMA × 100
Visual Representation Green pill (● Bullish +X%) when ≥ 0.
Coral pill (● Bearish -X%) when < 0.
Practical Application:
  • Bullish Regime: Price is supported by institutional capital inflows. Buy pullbacks to the 20-EMA or 50-SMA with confidence.
  • Bearish Regime: Price faces persistent institutional overhead supply. Avoid aggressive dip-buying; upside bounces are frequently sold off into liquidity exits.

⎯⎯ 20-Day Exponential Moving Average (20-EMA)

Live in Terminal
First Principle: Short-Term Collective Consensus & Memory. 20 trading sessions represent exactly one calendar month. The exponential weighting factor (k = 2 / (20 + 1) ≈ 9.52%) places double the mathematical significance on recent sessions compared to older bars, accurately reflecting the current price consensus of active market participants.
Color & Style Cyan dashed line (#38BDF8)
Role in Market Dynamic trailing support during rapid trend acceleration.
Practical Application: In a strong uptrend, pullbacks that touch or briefly pierce the 20-EMA and bounce are the classic "low-risk entry" for momentum investors. A decisive daily close below the 20-EMA is your earliest warning to lock in partial profits or tighten stop-losses.

⎯⎯ 50-Day Simple Moving Average (50-SMA)

Live in Terminal
First Principle: The Quarterly Institutional Benchmark. 50 trading days corresponds to approximately 10 calendar weeks — the exact interval of corporate quarterly earnings reports and fund quarterly rebalancing schedules. It represents the average price paid by institutional investors over the current corporate cycle.
Color & Style Amber solid line (#F59E0B)
Key Signal The intermediate trend filter separating healthy pullbacks from trend reversals.
Practical Application: In high-quality growth compounders, institutions defend the 50-day SMA aggressively. When a stock pulls back to a rising 50-day SMA on declining volume, it offers an optimal institutional risk/reward entry point.

⎯⎯ 200-Day Simple Moving Average (200-SMA)

Live in Terminal
First Principle: The Annual Secular Equilibrium Line. 200 trading sessions encompass approximately 40 weeks (10 calendar months). It filters out all short-term noise, earnings surprises, and quarterly volatility, revealing the true multi-year trajectory of the underlying enterprise.
Color & Style Purple solid line (#A855F7)
Milestone Events Golden Cross (50-SMA crosses above 200-SMA) · Death Cross (50-SMA crosses below 200-SMA).
Practical Application: Never bet against the slope of the 200-SMA. If the 200-SMA is sloping upward and price is above it, the long-term probabilities are heavily in your favor. If price is trapped below a declining 200-SMA, long investments must be treated as speculative turnarounds with strict position sizing.

Volume Histogram & Conviction Analysis

Live in Terminal
First Principle: Price is the Argument; Volume is the Verdict. Price movements on negligible volume represent retail order matching and dealer inventory adjustments. Price movements on towering volume represent irreversible institutional capital reallocation.
Bar Color Logic Green: Close ≥ Previous Close (Accumulation).
Red: Close < Previous Close (Distribution).
Architecture Plotted on an isolated secondary Y-axis scaled to the bottom 20% of the canvas so it never obscures price action.
Practical Application:
  • Institutional Breakout: A breakout to 52-week highs accompanied by 2x+ average volume confirms genuine demand.
  • Volume Exhaustion / Divergence: If price pushes to a higher high on significantly lower volume, buyer enthusiasm is drying up — anticipate a mean-reversion pullback.

⎯⎯ 14-Period Relative Strength Index (RSI 14)

Live in Terminal
First Principle: Measuring the Velocity and Internal Friction of Price Changes. Developed by J. Welles Wilder, the RSI quantifies the velocity of price movement by calculating the ratio of smoothed upward price changes to smoothed downward changes over 14 sessions, bounded between 0 and 100.
Reference Thresholds Overbought: RSI > 70 · Oversold: RSI < 30.
Sub-Panel Interface Dedicated oscillating canvas below price chart with dynamic color-coded value badges.
Practical Application:
  • In Ranging Markets: Buy when RSI touches or dips below 30; take profits when RSI crosses above 70.
  • In Strong Bull Regimes: Strong stocks often oscillate between 40 and 80. An RSI pullback to 40–45 in a bull regime is an aggressive buying opportunity, whereas 70 is simply evidence of strong momentum rather than immediate reversal.
  • Bullish Divergence: Price makes a lower low, but RSI forms a higher low. This indicates that seller conviction has evaporated before price turns around — one of the highest win-rate reversal setups in equity markets.

3. The 5-Step End-to-End Decision Framework

Combine the terminal's tools into an integrated, repeatable 5-step checklist before executing any stock or ETF trade:

Step Analytical Check Terminal Tool Decision Threshold
Step 1 Signal & Macro Alignment Signal Tag & Market Regime Verify that the summary signal tag displays BUY (or constructive HOLD) and Regime is Bullish (> 200 SMA). Avoid initiating new long exposure under an active SELL signal.
Step 2 Historical Cycle Reality Yearly Range Ledger & Revised % Verify whether historical cycles exhibit clean Low → High rallies. If recent years are flagged, demand a Revised Next-Year High potential of at least 2.5x your downside risk.
Step 3 Swing & Momentum Check 50 SMA & 20 EMA Overlays Ensure price is stabilizing above the rising 50-day SMA. Use the 20-day EMA to time low-risk pullback entries.
Step 4 Exhaustion & Divergence RSI (14) Sub-Panel Avoid buying when RSI > 75. Look for entries when RSI is recovering out of oversold territory (< 35) or bouncing off 45 in a bull regime. Watch for bullish divergences.
Step 5 Institutional Conviction Volume Histogram Ensure accumulation sessions show taller green volume bars than red pullback sessions. Breakouts must occur on expanding volume.
Asymmetric Risk/Reward Sizing

Use the terminal's Diff from Latest Low and Diff from All-Time High metrics to set realistic stop-losses. If current price is $100, the 52-week low is $92, and the conservative revised target is $124, your downside risk is $8 and your upside target is $24 — delivering an institutional 3.0:1 Reward-to-Risk ratio.
